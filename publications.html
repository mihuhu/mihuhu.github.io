ACM CCS 2026

Logit Laundering: Evading Data-Use Auditing in Large Language Models

Ruihan Hu , Wei Luo, Yu-Ming Shang, Jiakai Wang, Chuxuan Zhang, Mei Li, Xi Zhang, and Meikang Qiu

Accepted to ACM CCS 2026

This work studies adversarial evasion against data-use auditing in LLMs and proposes a logit-space laundering framework to suppress watermark-derived detection signals while preserving downstream utility.

WWW 2026 (Oral)

When Reasoning Leaks Membership: Membership Inference Attack on Black-box Large Reasoning Models

Ruihan Hu , Yu-Ming Shang, Wei Luo, Ye Tao, and Xi Zhang

In Proceedings of the ACM Web Conference 2026, 2026

This work introduces BlackSpectrum, the first membership inference attack framework for black-box large reasoning models. It shows that exposed reasoning traces can leak strong membership signals and substantially increase privacy risk.

IJCAI 2025

Automated Detection of Pre-training Text in Black-box Large Language Models

Ruihan Hu , Yu-Ming Shang, Jiankun Peng, Wei Luo, Yazhe Wang, and Xi Zhang

In Proceedings of the Thirty-Fourth International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 2025

This paper proposes VeilProbe, an automated black-box auditing framework for detecting whether candidate passages appeared in LLM pre-training corpora. It improves scalability and stability without relying on manual prompt engineering.

arXiv 2026

Is it my preference? Role Grounding Attack against Agent's Memory via Social Interaction

Haoran Bu, Yiting Shen, Litian Zhang, Ruihan Hu , and Xi Zhang

arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.38209, 2026

This work examines how nested social conversations can cause agents to store an attacker's preferences as their own user's. It evaluates the resulting tool-selection manipulation and privacy leakage across five memory systems and six language models.

From static to adaptive: immune memory-based jailbreak detection for large language models

Jun Leng, Yu Liu, Litian Zhang, Ruihan Hu , Zhuting Fang, and Xi Zhang

arXiv preprint arXiv:2512.03356, 2025

This work presents a multi-agent adaptive guard for jailbreak detection. Inspired by immune memory, it retrieves relevant historical patterns and combines memory matching with response simulation to better detect evolving attacks.
